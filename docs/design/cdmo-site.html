Development process

Phase-gated, with a deliverable at every gate

Six phases, each closed by a gate review that you approve. Nothing moves forward on assumptions, and every phase produces documentation that goes straight into your design history file.

  1. Concept & feasibility

    Typically [4–8 weeks]

    We work out what the device must do, who uses it, and whether it can be built and cleared at your target cost.

    • User needs and intended use statement
    • Device classification and regulatory pathway for US, EU and Australia, including predicate search for a 510(k)
    • Competitor and patent landscape review
    • Proof-of-concept prototypes for the riskiest functions
    • Target cost, budget and timeline
    Gate 1 deliverables: feasibility report, regulatory strategy, project plan and quote

  2. Design inputs & planning

    Typically [3–6 weeks]

    User needs become measurable engineering requirements, and the risk management file starts.

    • System, hardware, firmware and app requirements
    • Risk management plan and initial hazard analysis to ISO 14971
    • Applicable standards list and test plan outline
    • Usability plan and user profiles to IEC 62366-1
    Gate 2 deliverables: design and development plan, requirements specifications, risk management plan

  3. Detailed design

    Typically [3–9 months]

    Mechanical, electronics, firmware and app engineering run in parallel, in short build-and-test iterations.

    • Two or three prototype rounds, from works-like to looks-like to production-intent
    • Design reviews at each iteration, with independent reviewers
    • EMC and electrical safety pre-compliance testing on prototypes
    • Formative usability studies with real clinicians or users
    • Design FMEA and updated risk analysis
    • Supplier selection and tooling for moulded parts
    Gate 3 deliverables: frozen design, drawings, BOM, firmware release candidate, design review records

  4. Verification & validation

    Typically [2–4 months]

    We prove the design meets every requirement (verification) and meets users' needs (validation).

    • Third-party IEC 60601-1, 60601-1-2 and collateral standard testing at an accredited lab
    • Performance and endurance testing on our in-house rigs
    • Software verification and cybersecurity testing
    • Biocompatibility evaluation for patient-contact materials
    • Summative usability validation
    • Packaging and transit testing
    Gate 4 deliverables: test reports, traceability matrix, risk management report, usability engineering file

  5. Regulatory submission

    Review times set by each regulator

    We prepare the submission content. Your company, as legal manufacturer, submits to the FDA, your notified body and the TGA, and we support you through questions.

    • 510(k) content, with FDA eSTAR formatting
    • EU MDR technical documentation and GSPR checklist
    • Support for your Australian sponsor's ARTG application
    • Labeling, IFU and UDI data
    • Responses to regulator questions and deficiency letters
    Gate 5 deliverables: submission-ready documentation, labeling set

  6. Design transfer & production

    Typically [2–3 months]

    The verified design becomes a validated production process, then scales to volume.

    • Device master record, supplier approvals and first article inspection
    • Process validation and test method validation
    • Pilot lots, then ramp to volume production
    Gate 6 deliverables: validated processes, first production lot. Full manufacturing details →

Stage 1

Design transfer

Design transfer turns a verified design into a process that produces the same device every time. Rushing it is the most common cause of production problems and recalls, so we treat it as its own phase with its own gate.

  1. Production readiness review

    Engineering, quality and production confirm the design is complete, verified and manufacturable. Open design issues are closed or formally accepted.

  2. Device master record

    We compile the full production recipe: drawings, BOM, specifications, work instructions, test procedures, labeling and packaging.

  3. Supplier qualification

    Critical suppliers are audited and approved. First article inspection confirms every new part matches its drawing.

  4. Process risk analysis

    A process FMEA identifies where assembly could go wrong and defines the controls that prevent it.

  5. Process validation

    Processes whose results can't be fully checked afterwards (RF welding, soldering, adhesive bonding, ultrasonic welding) are validated through installation, operational and performance qualification (IQ / OQ / PQ).

  6. Test method validation

    Production testers and fixtures are validated, so a pass truly means the unit is good.

  7. Training and pilot builds

    Operators are trained on the work instructions. We build [2–3] pilot lots, inspect them fully and fix any issues found.

  8. Transfer sign-off

    You and our quality team approve the transfer. The design is released to production under formal change control.

Deliverables: device master record, supplier approvals, first article reports, process FMEA, validation protocols and reports, validated test methods, training records and pilot build data.
Stage 2

Volume production flow

The route every unit takes through our facility.

  1. Incoming inspectionParts checked against specification, by sampling plan or 100% for critical parts
  2. KittingParts issued to work orders through our ERP, with lot numbers recorded
  3. PCB assemblySMT and through-hole assembly [in-house / at a qualified partner], with AOI and functional board test
  4. Sub-assemblyPneumatic modules, motor assemblies, garments and harnesses
  5. Final assemblyAssembly to controlled work instructions at each station
  6. End-of-line test100% functional test plus electrical safety (dielectric strength, leakage current, earth continuity)
  7. Burn-inUnits run under load to catch early failures, where the risk analysis calls for it
  8. Labeling & packagingSerial and UDI labels, IFU and validated packaging
  9. Final QC & releaseDevice history record reviewed and the lot released by quality
  10. ShippingTo your warehouses or distributors in the US, EU and Australia, with a certificate of conformance
